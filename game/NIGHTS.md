# Moonpetal Heist — a field guide to the ten nights

How each night is built, who walks it, and the trick that opens it.
Par times are the Swift Moth bar: finish under par and the seal is yours.

## Night I — Petal Court (par 75s, 5 petals, 2 keepers)

The court is quiet. Two keepers walk the lanterns on short loops, and
the moonpetals glow like small pink lamps. This is the night that teaches
the cone: watch one full patrol before you move, and learn that a bush
hides you unless a keeper is almost on top of you. The gate is at the far
end, past the second keeper's turn.

## Night II — Hedge Maze (par 95s, 7 petals, 3 keepers)

The hedges grow tall here and the paths turn back on themselves. Learn
the patrols before you trust a shortcut: three keepers cross the maze on
offset loops, so there is always one corridor that is empty for a few
seconds. Sneak through the middle hedge row — running is heard across the
whole maze.

## Night III — Koi Pond (par 110s, 3 petals, 3 keepers)

Koi drift under the pond and the keepers cross the bridges slowly. Water
hides nothing — stay off the open stone. The prize petal sits in a
lantern box at the bottom of the garden: the box is open on one side
only, and the keeper who walks past it turns slowly. Enter behind him,
take the petal, leave before he turns.

## Night IV — Lantern Row (par 120s, 5 petals, 4 keepers)

Every lantern is lit tonight and every path is bright. The dark between
the lanterns is thin. Move like a shadow between candles: the gaps
between the rows are the only cover, and the fourth keeper — a
lantern-bearer with a longer, wider beam — walks the middle row. His beam
is narrow at its edges; slip past his flank, never his front.

## Night V — Rose Vault (par 135s, 7 petals, 4 keepers)

The roses are thick and the bushes are deep. The bell-ringer walks here
now, and his bell is quicker than your wings: his suspicion fills half
again as fast as a keeper's. He walks alone, which is the weakness — a
pollen puff pulls him like any keeper, and the rose bushes hide you while
he stares at the glow. The vault's prize petal is ringed by lanterns;
take it from the dark side.

## Night VI — Bell Tower (par 150s, 4 petals, 5 keepers)

The tower bell hangs above the court. Five keepers, one bell, no place
to hide that is not already watched. This is the night that teaches the
puff: you cannot outwait five patrols, so you must spend the glow. Throw
a puff across the court, let two keepers turn to it, and cross the gap
they leave. The gate is far from the last petal — plan the exit before
you take it.

## Night VII — Moon Gate (par 180s, 7 petals, 6 keepers)

The Moon Gate stands open at the far end of the garden. Take every
petal, pass every keeper, and vanish before dawn. Six keepers, including
a night watcher whose beam sweeps like a slow lighthouse across the
court — the sweep is a rhythm, and the rhythm has a gap. Cross while it
looks the other way. The gate itself is lit: the last twenty paces are
the brightest in the garden.

## Night VIII — Moonwell (par 165s, 5 petals, 4 keepers)

Past the gate, a second garden. Cold moonwell pools light a moth like a
stage, and a hound hunts by scent. The pools are the walls here: the
court is cut by blue water, and the only dry crossings are behind the
hedges. The hound patrols the pool's edge with its violet scent-cone —
short, wide, and it only smells close. Throw your puff far, across the
water, and never stand where you threw from: the hound will come to that
spot, not to the glow.

## Night IX — Moonwell Deep (par 190s, 6 petals, 6 keepers)

The wells are everywhere now and the hounds run in pairs. Two scent-
cones overlap at every crossing, so a puff thrown carelessly brings two
noses instead of one. The trick is the veil: one charge a night, six
seconds of silk, dropped at the far crossing while both hounds follow a
puff you threw the other way. The petals here are worth the risk — the
gate is at the top of the garden, past the deepest pool.

## Night X — The Last Petal (par 210s, 7 petals, 8 keepers)

The last petal grows in the middle of everything: every keeper, every
hound, every well, one garden. Eight guards walk the final court — the
most the garden has ever held — and the last petal sits in a lantern box
in the open, lit by the moon gate itself. There is no single trick left:
spend every puff, every veil, every lesson. Take it, and the whole
garden is yours.
